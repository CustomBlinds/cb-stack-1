Blinds in George: Choosing Window Coverings That Suit the Garden Route Climate

George sits at the centre of the Garden Route, and its weather is unlike anywhere else in South Africa. Winters are cold and genuinely wet, summer afternoons bring strong low sun off the mountains, and homes closer to the coast take salt-laden air year round. Window blinds chosen for a home in Johannesburg or Cape Town do not always cope with those conditions, which is why George homeowners are better served planning around the local climate from the start.

Which Blinds Suit a George Home

For the wet Garden Route winter, moisture resistance matters more than fashion. Aluminium venetian blinds and quality roller blinds handle humidity and condensation far better than untreated timber, which can swell and warp in a damp lounge or bathroom. For bedrooms, blockout roller blinds hold back the bright summer mornings, while honeycomb blinds add a layer of insulation that keeps the winter chill off the glass. Homes with large mountain-facing or sea-facing windows often combine a sheer and a blockout on a double roller so light can be tuned through the day.

Why George Homeowners Choose Made-to-Measure

Off-the-shelf blinds are cut to standard sizes that rarely match an older George home or a newer build with wide aluminium frames. A made-to-measure blind is cut to the exact window recess, which removes the light gaps down the sides and gives a cleaner finish. Made-to-measure also opens up the full range of fabrics and mechanisms, so a homeowner is not forced to compromise on colour or light control just to find something that fits.

Getting the Measurement Right

Accurate measurement is the single biggest factor in a blind that looks and works well. A few millimetres decide whether a blind sits flush in the recess or leaves a gap. Measuring the width in three places and the drop in three places, then working to the smallest figure, is the simplest way to avoid a poor fit, and it is worth doing before ordering any blind for a George window.
