Vertical Blinds in South Africa: A Practical Guide for Large Windows and Sliding Doors

Published 3 August 2026

Vertical blinds are one of the most practical window treatments for the way South African homes and offices are actually built. Wide aluminium sliding doors, floor to ceiling glass and long office windows all share a problem: horizontal treatments sag or strain across the span, and heavy curtains swallow the light. Vertical vanes solve that by hanging from a single head track and rotating in place, so a three metre run is as easy to manage as a small window.

How they work

A vertical blind is a row of individual vanes suspended from a track. A wand or cord tilts every vane at once to set the angle of light, and the whole set draws to one side or splits to the centre to clear the glass completely. Because the vanes move vertically, dust settles less on them than on horizontal slats, and a stray knock simply swings a vane back into place rather than bending it.

Choosing the vane material

Fabric vanes give a soft, warm finish and are the usual choice for lounges, bedrooms and boardrooms. Sunscreen fabrics hold the view and cut glare while still filtering strong coastal light, which suits north and west facing rooms. Where moisture is a factor, such as a kitchen or a bathroom, a PVC vane wipes clean and resists warping. For a room that needs to go properly dark, a blockout fabric vane is the one to specify. Aluminium and timber are common on horizontal blinds but are rarely used as vertical vanes, so the real decision here is fabric weight and openness rather than a hard material.

Getting the size right

Vertical blinds reward accurate measuring more than almost any other treatment, because a long track shows every millimetre of error. Decide first whether the blind sits inside the reveal for a neat, built in look or outside it to cover the frame and block side light. Large sliding doors usually take an outside fit so the stack clears the opening. It is also worth planning which side the vanes stack to, so they do not pile up in front of the door handle you use most.

Light, privacy and heat

The strength of a vertical blind is fine control. Tilt the vanes a little and you keep the view while taking the edge off the glare. Tilt them fully and you have privacy without losing all the daylight. On a hot facade, angling the vanes away from the sun through the afternoon noticeably reduces the heat load coming through the glass, which matters in a country where west facing rooms can become uncomfortable by late day.

Keeping them looking new

Upkeep is simple. A monthly dust with a soft cloth or the brush head of a vacuum keeps fabric vanes fresh, and PVC vanes take a damp wipe. If a vane is ever knocked off its carrier it clips straight back on, and single vanes can be replaced without redoing the whole blind, which keeps a large installation economical over its life.
